# A designer's peer critique

Reviewed the actual B/C SVG paths and their small variants. Parent-rendered pixel sheets are still required; these are geometry/craft judgments, not user-test results or similarity clearance.

## B · Fold

The most abstract, object-like direction. A compact mass with a single diagonal is a useful contrast to both the literal d and a conversational curve. It should work well as a quiet PWA tile and avoids the current record/target association.

The thin negative seam is the critical detail. At 16px its 1px opening and a 10px outer glyph are more fragile than A's enlarged 12×14px favicon. Compare the 16px raster at actual size; if the seam softens, increase it optically rather than adding an outline or color. The surrounding padded app art is intentionally small at that size, so the small.svg variant is essential. A solid square/corner can also read as a generic document or a cropped window; the name link is weaker than A.

## C · Conversation loop

The broad curve is the closest to Soft Focus's softer interaction language. One path is easy to maintain, and the open shape is calmer than stacked chat bubbles. The small variant has enough centerline separation on paper to survive 16px.

The strongest concern is interpretation: the return on the lower right makes it resemble a stylized G or navigation/refresh control. That is a more active visual instruction than a quiet app identifier. Test the stroke at actual favicon size: the inward horizontal terminal is a second event that can dominate the open loop, especially in reversal. Its stroke-based geometry is editable, but final canonical delivery should consider outlined geometry if rendering consistency across favicon pipelines becomes an issue. Do not add a second chat cue to explain it; retain the one-line mechanism or reject it.

## A · Self-critique

The custom d is the easiest to attribute to the product name and the least visually busy, but it is a crowded monogram category. Its small chamfer is deliberately modest and may disappear at 24px. That makes A a practical icon, not a dramatic brand symbol. Use its optical favicon variant; the installed-app padding should not be scaled down unchanged.
