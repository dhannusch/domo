# Brand strategy reference

This is a compact decision guide, not a copied framework manual. Use the linked sources for deeper
context when a decision depends on them.

## Ground the brand in a real situation

Describe:

- who is trying to make progress;
- what is happening before they look for help;
- the functional, social, and emotional progress they want;
- what they do instead today;
- what would make the choice credible;
- what the brand must not promise.

This is the useful part of Jobs to Be Done: it keeps the brand attached to a situation and desired
progress rather than a generic persona. See the [Christensen Institute overview](https://www.christenseninstitute.org/theory/jobs-to-be-done/).

## Make tradeoffs visible

Evaluate a direction on three separate questions:

- desirability: would the intended audience care?
- viability: could the business sustain it?
- feasibility: can this person or product credibly deliver it?

Do not let visual appeal hide a weak promise or an impossible proof burden. See [IDEO's design-thinking
overview](https://designthinking.ideo.com/).

## Minimum strategy record

The foundation should contain:

1. audience situations and jobs;
2. category and alternatives;
3. positioning sentence;
4. promise and proof;
5. personality and voice;
6. message hierarchy;
7. assumptions and open questions;
8. claims that require evidence;
9. decisions and rejected alternatives.

Use plain language. A brand strategy that cannot be explained in a few concrete sentences is not ready
to drive design.
