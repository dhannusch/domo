# Claims, naming, and rights

This reference keeps brand work useful without pretending that an agent has provided legal clearance.

## Claims ledger

Record each externally checkable claim with:

- the exact claim;
- source URL or supplied evidence;
- source date when relevant;
- confidence: `high`, `medium`, or `low`;
- status: `proposed`, `verified`, `needs-review`, or `retired`;
- owner and next review date when the claim can change.

Objective marketing claims need substantiation before publication. See the [FTC advertising
substantiation policy](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/ftc-policy-statement-regarding-advertising-substantiation).

## Preliminary name and mark checks

For a proposed name or logo, perform an advisory preflight:

- exact and close spelling;
- sound-alike and plural forms;
- visual or semantic similarity;
- relevant goods and services;
- target-country registries where applicable;
- domain and social availability only as supporting evidence.

Use the [USPTO trademark search](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search/federal-trademark-searching)
and [WIPO Global Brand Database](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/global-brand-database/index), then label
the result `unverified`, `potential-conflict`, or `needs-attorney-review`. Never call it cleared.

## AI and provenance

Keep a provenance record for generated or sourced assets. The record should include the source,
creator or model, date, prompt or reference, license, commercial-use posture, and a content hash when
available. Human selection, arrangement, and modification should be recorded when authorship matters.

Do not send confidential product details, customer data, or unreleased strategy to an external image
backend. Do not copy or trace a recognizable existing mark.
