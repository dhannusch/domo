# Copy and voice reference

Use this as a quality bar, not a style to imitate. The links are the source material; this file is an
original summary of the decisions that matter for Domo's brand work.

## Voice versus tone

Voice is the stable character of the brand. Tone changes with the reader's situation. Keep the voice
recognizable while making the tone calmer during uncertainty, more direct during action, and warmer
when welcoming or thanking someone.

Sources: [Microsoft brand voice](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/style-guide/brand-voice-above-all-simple-human)
and [Mailchimp voice and tone](https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/voice-and-tone/).

## Write for the reader's next decision

- Make the first sentence useful.
- Prefer concrete nouns and active verbs.
- Name the audience's situation before describing the product.
- Replace adjectives with evidence, examples, or an observable outcome.
- Explain specialist language the first time it matters.
- Remove hype words unless the claim is specific and defensible.
- Give each piece one primary job.

The [GOV.UK writing guidance](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/design/writing-for-user-interfaces)
and [ONS user-needs guidance](https://service-manual.ons.gov.uk/content/writing-for-users/user-needs)
are useful references for clarity, structure, and user-centered wording.

## Review questions

Before delivery, ask:

1. Does the reader know whether this is for them?
2. Is the promise specific enough to disagree with?
3. Is every objective claim supported or marked as a hypothesis?
4. Does the copy sound like the approved voice rather than generic model prose?
5. Is the call to action the smallest useful next step?
